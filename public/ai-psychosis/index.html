What the government filed

In Document 56, the United States says its earlier request is now “entirely a motion for a competency evaluation pursuant to 18 U.S.C. § 4241.” The filing argues that Channon's use of generative AI tools in developing litigation strategy is evidence relevant to competency.

Why this is newsworthy

Federal prosecutors have cited a defendant’s use of ChatGPT and Claude as evidence of “AI-induced psychosis” and asked a judge to question whether he is mentally fit to stand trial.

Channon is already under a court-ordered counseling regime, currently twice a month, and the provider’s own written assessment — available below — reports findings sharply at odds with that portrayal.

Yet prosecutors are seeking a competency examination that can place him in federal custody and a psychiatric facility for up to 30 days — potentially 45 with an extension. That makes this more than an unusual AI story. It is also a question of government waste and judicial oversight: what justifies escalating psychiatric intervention when the court-mandated treatment record is already producing contrary evidence?
